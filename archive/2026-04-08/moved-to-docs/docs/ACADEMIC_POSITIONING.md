# Academic Positioning: AI-HPP as a Verification Standard

## Purpose
This document positions AI-HPP in relation to established AI documentation and governance artifacts used in academia, industry, and regulatory practice. AI-HPP should be interpreted as a **verification Standard** and operational **Protocol**, rather than an ethical manifesto.

## AI-HPP vs Model Cards
**Model Cards** primarily describe model characteristics, intended use, and known limitations. They improve transparency at the model level.

AI-HPP differs by focusing on:
- Requirement-level verification obligations.
- System-level controls beyond a single model artifact.
- Audit Artifact generation tied to measurable Verification Checkpoints.

In short, Model Cards are descriptive disclosures; AI-HPP is a conformance-oriented verification Framework.

## AI-HPP vs Datasheets for Datasets
**Datasheets for Datasets** provide structured documentation for dataset provenance, curation decisions, and potential biases.

AI-HPP differs by:
- Covering the entire agentic lifecycle, not only data assets.
- Including runtime control, tool-execution boundaries, incident handling, and governance escalation.
- Requiring verifiable evidence links from requirements to operational records.

Datasheets support dataset accountability; AI-HPP supports end-to-end verification accountability.

## AI-HPP vs ISO Documentation
**ISO documentation** typically aligns organizations to certifiable management-system processes and control evidence, often at an enterprise governance level.

AI-HPP differs by:
- Defining AI-specific normative requirements for agentic behavior and safety controls.
- Embedding threat/incident-grounded rationale directly in requirement design.
- Providing machine-readable evidence schemas for technical audit portability.

AI-HPP can complement ISO-aligned management systems but is not a replacement for broader organizational certification.

## AI-HPP vs AI Governance Whitepapers
**AI governance whitepapers** usually present principles, recommendations, and policy narratives.

AI-HPP differs by:
- Translating governance intent into testable requirements.
- Requiring explicit verification logic and evidence production.
- Enabling repeatable inspection workflows rather than high-level advisory interpretation.

Whitepapers motivate governance direction; AI-HPP operationalizes governance verification.

## Positioning Statement
AI-HPP is best understood as a **verification Standard for agentic AI systems** that integrates technical controls, governance obligations, and auditable evidence into a single conformance-oriented Protocol. It is not an ethical manifesto and does not rely on aspirational language as the primary assurance mechanism.
